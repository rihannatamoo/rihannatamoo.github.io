EVIDENCE 01 · AI CUSTOMER INTELLIGENCE · ENTERPRISE SAAS

From a fuzzy AI vision to a coherent customer intelligence product model.

The challenge was not simply to add AI. It was to define what each layer of the experience should mean, where AI creates new value, how users verify the insight and what engineering should actually build.

THE PRODUCT PROBLEM

The product already had signals. AI risked adding more labels without adding more clarity.

Users could already work with known topics, subtopics, sentiment and customer comments. Proposed AI concepts introduced themes, discovery candidates and additional keyword behavior. Several ideas sounded useful in isolation, but they overlapped when viewed as one product.

The product question became more precise: what belongs to the known taxonomy, what is genuinely emerging, how does the user move from pattern to evidence, and what must remain explainable?

The distinction I protected: topic analysis explains what customers are saying about categories the business already tracks. Theme discovery surfaces patterns the taxonomy may not yet capture. Verbatim insight gives the customer evidence behind either signal.
WHAT I OWNED

I translated a broad future state into a product model teams could reason about and build.

01

Product architecture

Defined the roles of topics, subtopics, keywords, theme candidates and verbatim evidence so each capability had a clear job.

02

Signal to evidence flow

Structured the experience so users can move from aggregate patterns into the supporting customer comments instead of trusting an opaque AI output.

03

Behavior and defaults

Specified page context, ranking logic, search behavior, sentiment filtering, cluster behavior, exports, permissions and key default states.

04

Delivery definition

Converted product decisions and mockups into functional requirements, business rules, acceptance criteria and system expectations for engineering and QA.

WHY THIS MATTERS

AI product leadership is often a clarity problem before it becomes a model problem.

Without clear capability boundaries, teams can ship overlapping features that confuse users and create implementation debt. This work reduces that risk by defining the user value, the information model and the expected behavior together.
